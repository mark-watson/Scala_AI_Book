Solar power turns sunlight into electricity with no exhaust. A home
array with a small battery can run lights, heat pumps, and an electric
car charger through the night. Each kilowatt hour from rooftop solar
replaces a kilowatt hour from gas or coal plants, which cuts carbon
dioxide output. Net metering lets homes sell spare power back to the
grid on sunny days. Install cost has dropped for ten straight years,
so payback now lands near seven years in most US states.

Wind farms add night power when solar fades. Grid batteries store
spare noon solar for the evening peak. Together, solar, wind, and
storage let towns retire peaker plants that once ran on fossil gas.
